01 / About

From molecular biology to engineered immune systems.

My work sits at the intersection of chemical engineering, molecular biology, and immunology. I am interested in building experimental systems that make complex cell behavior easier to measure, compare, and understand.

My research focuses on spatially organizing immune-cell interactions and capturing functional heterogeneity at the level of individual cellular interactions. I combine microfabrication, DNA-based patterning, cell culture, fluorescence assays, and analytical modeling.

02 / Research

A platform for seeing the heterogeneity hidden in averages.

Conventional measurements can compress diverse cellular responses into a single population-level value. My research explores spatially organized assays designed to preserve information about individual immune-cell interactions.

DNA-PATTERNED ARRAY
SPATIAL CAPTURE · CELL INTERACTION · SIGNAL READOUT
01

DNA-directed spatial organization

Addressable DNA interfaces are used to organize molecular capture regions and create defined spatial locations for downstream measurements.

02

One-on-one immune interactions

Patterned platforms provide a framework for examining individual dendritic-cell/T-cell interactions and their heterogeneous functional responses.

03

Cytokine capture and phenotyping

Localized cytokine measurements are paired with cellular activation phenotyping to connect secreted signals with cellular behavior.
